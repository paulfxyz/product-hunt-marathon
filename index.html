§ 01 What this actually is

Build. Share. Keep going.

A public experiment in what one person can make with AI, what it costs, and what happens after people start using it.

01

Small on purpose.

Useful projects with a clear job to do. AI helped with design and code; the product choices, testing and responsibility are mine.

02

Code to take further.

The sprint has open-source roots: code to inspect, extend or commercialise under its licence. One project grew beyond the original experiment into a freemium app. That commercial product and its MIT foundation are different things.

03

Costs, without the fairy tale.

These builds often cost hundreds, not a handful of dollars. The budgets and model stacks below are labelled draft examples until I replace them with actual usage and spend.

The experiment continues

Shipping is the start.
Feedback is what follows.

In the days after launch, I’ll consider every bug report, request and suggestion. I’ll weigh the value, the smallest useful change and the AI-compute cost, then do my best to fund quick iterations myself.

01 · Listen to real use

Tell me what broke, what got in your way, or what could make the project more useful.

02 · Fix what matters

Bugs first. Then meaningful, manageable improvements rather than a growing list of half-built features.

03 · Improve it together

Use public feedback to choose the next iteration, and be open about progress, limits and cost.

Every request considered. Not every feature promised, and no guaranteed turnaround. Share feedback through the project’s repository or the launch conversation.
